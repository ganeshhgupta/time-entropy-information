# Bibliography — in the order it entered the reasoning

This is not a topical reference list — it's the actual research trajectory:
every paper/source in the order it was pulled in, why, and what happened to
it under adversarial review. Status tags follow the scheme fixed in
`self_critique.md`'s meta-lesson:

- **VERIFIED** — independently confirmed (by a dedicated check, not just a
  reviewer's say-so) to exist with the stated identifier and to support the
  claim attached to it.
- **REPORTED** — cited by a reviewer agent or the original source material;
  not yet independently re-checked against the primary source.
- **RETRACTED** — checked and found wrong (fabricated, misattributed, or
  cited for a claim it doesn't make). Kept in this list, not deleted, so the
  mistake stays visible.

## Stage 5 — round 4: the computational round (toy models)

New sources pulled in while building `toy_model_detector_recorder.py`,
`toy_model_tape_recorder.py`, and `toy_model_qrf_invariant.py`.

45. "Reliable quantum master equation of the Unruh-DeWitt detector,"
    arXiv:2502.06411 — **VERIFIED** (round 4, found via live search and its
    title/abstract content directly confirms the claim): the Unruh-DeWitt
    detector, weakly coupled to a scalar field, is well-modeled at late
    times by a GKSL/Lindblad master equation driving it toward the Gibbs
    state at the Unruh temperature. This is the citation grounding the
    toy model's entire level of approximation — load-bearing.
46. Ciccarello, Lorenzo, Giovannetti & Palma, "Quantum collision models:
    open system dynamics from repeated interactions," Phys. Rep. 954, 1–70
    (2022), arXiv:2106.11974 — **VERIFIED** (round 4, confirmed via search
    directly against the Physics Reports record): the standard
    "repeated-interaction" / fresh-ancilla architecture used to justify the
    toy model's periodic-recorder-reset mechanism (condition c).
47. de la Hamette, arXiv:2603.23598 (already VERIFIED to exist, Stage 2
    item 41) — **re-checked this round at the level of its actual theorem**,
    not just its abstract: fetched directly via WebFetch, confirming the
    precise statement `S_α(Δρ_X^{(Ri)}) = S_α(Δρ_Y^{(Rj)})` for ideal
    frames, where Δ is dephasing in the group-label basis. The paper itself
    contains **no worked example** — confirmed directly, not assumed — which
    is why the round-4 QRF toy model is original rather than a
    reproduction, and also why its test of the theorem could not be
    cross-checked against the paper's own illustration.

**Lesson applied from round 1, successfully this time:** before writing
code that depended on de la Hamette's exact formula, the formula was
fetched and read directly rather than reused from round-1's reviewer
paraphrase ("diagonal Rényi invariants... bound the disagreement") — which,
on inspection, was accurate in spirit but not precise enough to implement
correctly on its own. Fetching the primary source before building on a
citation, not just before asserting a prose claim, is now treated as the
same discipline, not a separate one.
